# Data provenance: why SUM(quantity_sold) is 89,232 and not 88,481

Generated by `tools/provenance_may2024.py`. Every figure here is asserted by
that script and by `verify_data.py`; neither is a hand-typed number.

## The decomposition

`USTore_sales_long_with_zeros.csv` minus `USTore_sales_long_May_Aug2024-May2026.csv`,
by month. Exactly two months move; every other month totals the same to the unit.

| Month | Old file | Zero-filled | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2024-05 | 4318 | 4022 | **-296** |
| 2026-07 | 0 | 1047 | **+1047** |
| **Net** | | | **+751** |

Of the 25 months present, 23 are unchanged.

## 2026-07 is a new month

The old combined CSV ends at 2026-06-30. The zero-fill rebuild picked up a
July 2026 sheet the old file never had: +1,047 units. Nothing subtle here.

## 2024-05 is a re-sourcing, not new data

This is the part worth reading. **Both files cover the same 23 tally dates**
in May 2024 - the month did not gain or lose a single observation date. What
changed is which sheet of `2024 5 MAY DSR & TBS.xlsx` the month was read from.

- The old converter (`Converter Aug 2024 - May 2026.py`) accepts a sheet only if
  its name ends in `- TBS` (`is_tbs_month_sheet`). The May 2024 workbook's tally
  sheet is named plain `TBS`, and the workbook is not in that converter's
  `default_files` list either. It could not have supplied May 2024 by that path.
- The old CSV's May 2024 therefore came from the workbook's 23 daily
  `DAILY SALES REPORT` sheets (`May 2`, `May 3`, ...). Those label items
  **without a price** and carry separate `RETAIL PRICE`, `DISCOUNTED PRICE` and
  `SPECIAL DISC. PRICE` columns, each with its own `PCS SOLD`.
- `step0_convert_sales_with_zeros.py` hardcodes `sheet_names = ["TBS"]` for this
  workbook, and the TBS sheet **folds the price into the item label**.

### The sticker signature

The clearest fingerprint of the two labelling conventions:

| Source | Labels | Units |
|---|---|---:|
| DSR daily sheets | one unpriced `Sticker` | **336** |
| TBS sheet | `Sticker @ 115` + `Sticker @20` + `Long Sticker` + `Sticker Pack` | **65** |

32 DSR labels have no TBS counterpart and 28 TBS labels have no DSR counterpart;
the item-level detail is in `may2024_dsr_vs_tbs.csv`.

### Where the 296 units sit

The DSR sheets split their quantities across three price channels:

| Channel | Units |
|---|---:|
| DISCOUNTED | 551 |
| RETAIL | 3719 |
| SPECIAL DISC. | 48 |
| **DSR total** | **4318** |
| **TBS total** | **4022** |

The DSR channels sum to **4318**, which is exactly the old CSV's May 2024 figure.
That equality is asserted, and it is what closes the argument: the old series
did not merely resemble the DSR sheets, it *was* the DSR sheets.

The TBS sheet has no equivalent breakdown - it records one number per priced
SKU per date. That the DSR carries 599 units outside its retail channel is the
leading explanation for the gap, but confirming it is a question for USTore
staff, not for this script. It is logged as **B7** in the deferred-decision
register and is deliberately left open.

## What this means for Chapter 4

The manuscript's **88,481** was computed on a mixed-provenance series: May 2024
read from daily DSR sheets, every other month read from TBS tally sheets.
**89,232** is the single-provenance figure - every month read from the TBS
tally sheets, plus the July 2026 month the old file predated.

89,232 is the correct invariant. It is asserted in `verify_data.py`,
`tools/assert_invariants.py` and this script. See Divergence Register #18.
